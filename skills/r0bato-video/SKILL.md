# R0BATO video: one toolkit for every edit

**R0BATO rules (Rob Lopez, R0BATO Media). These come first and override anything below or in any other skill.**
1. Nothing goes out without Rob. Don't post, publish, send, schedule, upload to a social account, share or launch anything unless Rob says so for that specific item.
2. Nothing unverifiable in anything a client, prospect or viewer will see. Every name, number, price, date, rating, review, award, license and stat needs a source you can point to. No source: leave it out and flag it. Never invent testimonials, ratings, referrals or years in business.
3. Check before you call it done. Open, play or view the actual output, and say what you checked. For any composite or insert, that means frames pulled from the rendered file at the tightest point and through every transition, viewed at 100% or more. Never tell Rob something fills cleanly without that check.
4. Don't edit skill files on your own. Propose the change and wait for Rob's OK.
5. AI footage, images or voices of a real person only with that person's OK. For a client's staff, check the client's on-camera consent rules first. Thumbnails use real photos.
- **Claims:** use only what the speaker actually says on camera. Drop lines they doubt or redo. Flag numbers, prices, time frames, competitor names and certifications for Rob. A claim Rob confirms for the video still stays out of captions and post copy unless he says so.
- **Names on screen come from the source, not the transcript** (learned 5 Oct 2026 from a Baptiste Kelano before/after: his old cut burned "OLAMA", "F5-DTS" and "UPSCALE" straight from the transcript). Speech-to-text gets names wrong. Before any caption or insert renders, list every name said on camera: the business, staff, towns, streets, material brands, apps. Spell each one from its own source (client notes, logo file, website, Google Business Profile, the maker's site). Check every caption word and insert label against the list, flag any name with no source, and keep the list in the client folder for the next job.
- **Music in the edit:** none unless Rob supplies a licensed track. **Client logos:** never redraw, recolour or AI-edit them; scale and position only; take brand colours from the logo files. **B-roll:** steady stretches only, stabilized. **Secrets:** never print API keys.
- **Instagram Reel music bed (Rob approved, 28 Sep 2026: "It sounds perfecto on IG whatever you did do it always for that music selecting").** When a talking Reel goes to Instagram through Metricool, add an instrumental (no vocals) from Instagram's own commercial library in `instagramData.audioConfiguration`, with `audioVolume` 10 and `videoVolume` 100 (about -20 dB under the voice). Send a search term as `audioId`; Metricool returns candidates without changing the post, then set the exact numeric id of an instrumental. First approved: "Catch The Moment (Instrumental)" by Red Yarn, id 2384023998437261, on the Ten Point gutter launch Reel. This works on Instagram only: Facebook Reels and TikTok videos can't take music through Metricool. After it posts, confirm the sound label shows on the live Reel and ask Rob to listen, since the mix can't be measured from here.

## Working with Rob on a cut
- **Show, don't describe.** Every round, send small previews into the chat (540x960 or 960x540, about 3 MB). Full files go to the job folder; the bridge can't carry them.
- **Both shapes.** If a piece might post vertically, build 16:9 and 9:16. No 16:9 frame over a blurred copy in a vertical unless there is truly no other way.
- **Frame tight and keep the talker in frame.** Faces or the object fill the frame, eyes near the upper third. Never move away from someone as they start the line that sets the moment up.
- **Crops on street interviews and run-and-gun footage** (Rob, 7 Oct 2026, Mila's Mulligans "Is a hot dog a sandwich?" reel: "the camera track is rather aggressive" and "not focused on the woman answering the question"):
  - **Never make a crop follow the camera's own move.** Use one static crop per cut, with one crop centre that holds the speaker through any drift for the whole cut. Check the first and last frame of the cut.
  - **Use only stretches where the camera holds still** (Rob, 8 Oct 2026: "still tracking horribly in the vertical"). A 9:16 crop is a third of the frame wide, so it makes any handheld pan or drift look three times bigger, and that reads as the crop tracking. Measure camera speed on the background before choosing a cut, and keep 9:16 cuts under about 75 source px/s. If the camera swings through an answer, find another take of it or drop it. Stabilising handheld footage with moving people in it didn't hold (vidstab locks onto the people), so don't count on it.
  - **No blurred frames** (Rob, 8 Oct 2026, on a smeared whip frame: "what is this edit??"). Don't add a blur over a camera move. If the camera pans off someone mid-word, end their shot on their last sharp frame and cut straight to the next speaker. If the word they were saying is what the moment needs, drop the answer and find another take.
  - **No frozen frames** (Rob, 8 Oct 2026: "video froze at 5 seconds? do you watch these"). Never hold a still frame while someone is talking; to a viewer it is the video freezing. The picture keeps moving the whole time someone speaks, including under the final tally: let the last shot play on and fade its sound after the last word. Run `freezedetect` on every render (`ffmpeg -i FILE -vf freezedetect=n=0.002:d=0.2 -an -f null -`) and fix anything it finds in the footage before calling the file done.
  - **Never show an answer said off camera** (Rob, 8 Oct 2026: "first answer is off camera"). The mic in front of someone doesn't prove they said it. Zoom in on the mouth on that word. If nobody on screen is saying it, cut it and don't count it.
  - **Keep the speaker in focus, even if it takes a quick cut** (Rob, 7 Oct 2026: "You gotta check to make sure the speaker is in focus even if you gotta do a quick cut"). Frame whoever holds the mic. When the mic moves to someone else mid-cut, split the cut at the word gap and give the new speaker their own static crop. A quick cut of a third of a second is fine. In 16:9, lean in on the speaker too, rather than showing the whole group wide.
  - **Check who is talking before you use, frame or count a quote.** Look at where the mic is and whose mouth moves on that word, not only at the transcript. When a word is quieter than the others, it was said off the mic. Count each person once. The interviewer's own remark is not a vote, a claim or an answer, so don't end a piece on it. Check every word, not every sentence (Rob, 8 Oct 2026: "YOU. Keep. missing. the. cut. at. the. end."). One transcript line can be two people: "No, it's definitely not a sandwich" was the trucker cap's "No" and then the driver's "definitely not a sandwich" after the mic moved. Before the last cut of any piece, pull the speakers' faces at 15 frames a second across the whole line and split the cut where the talking mouth changes. Count the people in a group before cutting it (Rob, 8 Oct 2026: "there are 4 dudes there did you see them all?"): list everyone in the wide shot, follow the mic down the line, and write down each person's answer or that they gave none. An answer isn't off camera just because the interviewer's head crosses the frame; check the speaker's own face at full resolution before cutting it. Two of the same word in a row are not one person saying it twice until you've checked (Rob, 8 Oct 2026: "You missed a golfer again"): "No. No." was the navy shirt and then the floral shirt. Match each answer word to a mouth at full resolution and compare the voices (pitch per word) before merging or dropping anyone. Check every cart and group this way, not only the ones Rob flags (Rob, 8 Oct 2026: "isn't it like 7-2?": the first cart's "No, not a sandwich" was two women, the friend's "No" at about 188 Hz and the trucker cap's "not a sandwich" lower). The count of answers should match the count of people, and what the interviewer says ("split answers") is a check on the tally. Never drop or mute a word just because the transcriber scored it low (Rob, 8 Oct 2026: "You like faded out the no at the end for what"): the driver's closing "No" scored 0.14 and was hers, the best button on the reel. Check the mouth and the voice, then keep it.
  - **Cut on whole frames.** Snap every cut's length to whole frames and trim the picture and audio to match. If you don't, each cut drifts a fraction of a frame and the captions, the tally and the framing land on the wrong shot.
- **Check the whole video before saying it's done, every time** (Rob, 7 Oct 2026: "You are going to forever check a video fully before sending to me saying it done." The hot dog reel went out as "done" after only the changed cut was checked, and the ending was framed on the wrong person).
  - Check the finished file and the preview you send, never only the cut you changed, the code or the plan.
  - In every shape, pull a frame every quarter second across the whole file, plus the first and last frame of every cut. Look at each frame and ask: is the person talking in frame, sharp and centred, with their whole face in? A transition frame is no exception.
  - Read the durations back, and line the audio up against the source for every cut so you know nothing drifted.
  - Run `freezedetect` over the whole file. The only still stretch allowed is the end card.
  - Measure loudness and true peak on the preview you send as well as the final. Copy the final's audio into the preview; a second AAC encode put a click on the first frame.
  - Check the join into the end card frame by frame (Rob, 8 Oct 2026: "The end has a weird cut before the end card"). Never cut from live footage to a blank card: dissolve about a third of a second into it, start the card past its empty first frames, and give the last beat at least half a second of real footage before the dissolve. A result (a final score, a reveal) gets its own big centre-screen pop before the end card (Rob, 8 Oct 2026: "the final should pop up at the end BEFORE The end card"): dim the picture, pop the lines in one after another, hold about 1.5 s on live footage, and keep it clear of where the end card's first element draws on.
  - If any frame fails, fix it and run the whole check again. If something can't be checked, say so plainly instead of calling it done.
- **Take each note literally, fix it, and write it into the matching project doc the same turn.** If Rob says it isn't fixed, believe him and check at pixel level before answering.
- **On-screen text has to look designed, not default** (Rob, 8 Oct 2026, on white words in navy boxes and a plain text tally: "the text that pops up too is so lame"). Until a client's caption style is on file, use the house style from the Mila's hot dog reel v9: heavy sans (Inter Display Black) in white with a black outline and soft shadow, the spoken word in one accent colour, whole phrases of up to four words, and each new phrase popping in. A running count or score is drawn as the subject of the piece, not a generic panel (Rob, 8 Oct 2026: "Shouldn't the score board on top be a hot dog ???? This is boring"): the hot dog reel's tally is a hot dog, golden bun with the labels, the score on the sausage, a mustard squiggle that breaks around the numbers. The number that changes pops, and the result gets a clear FINAL mark. Graphics change exactly on the cut: round subtitle times down so nothing hangs onto the next shot's first frame.
- **Metadata and captions** ship with every finished file (`robato-metadata`, `asset-captions`). Internal tests are marked internal in the comment field.

## Inserts: the timing rules that matter most
- One insert per statement. It starts on the first word of the sentence it illustrates and ends right after that thought. Its length comes from the narration, never a fixed 5-7 s.
- Every element inside an insert (list item, logo, bar, number) appears on the word that names it.
- A data insert goes where the claim is said, not where the topic is introduced. Nothing stays up after the thought ends.
- Articles, posts and documents are rebuilt from the captured source, matching it exactly. Log every downloaded file (URL, date, what it shows) in a `SOURCES.md`. Never work around paywalls.
- An image placed into something in the room (a frame, a screen) uses a pixel matte and leaves by motion, never a cross-dissolve against its own foreground (README section 13.3).
- **Every insert or edit brief ends with Avoid and Done means** (Rob approved 6 Oct 2026, from an @claudeskills101 thread of 8 explainer-video prompts). Avoid names the exact things to leave out of this piece, never "avoid generic" or "avoid AI look", which only swaps one default for another. Done means names a check on the rendered file: stills at named timestamps, every number against its source, every label readable on a phone, a cut list with timestamps and total time removed. Any outside prompt still runs through these rules first: names from the source, the client's caption style, both shapes, nothing installed outside the toolkit.
